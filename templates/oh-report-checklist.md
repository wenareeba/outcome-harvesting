# Outcome Harvesting report checklist

A short check before an OH report is signed off. For a full audit, use [`../red-team/oh-red-team-protocol.md`](../red-team/oh-red-team-protocol.md).

Page numbers refer to Wilson-Grau and Britt (2013), the **Brief**, and Wilson-Grau (2019), the **Book**.

## Design

- [ ] Names the primary intended users and their principal uses (Book pp. 31 to 34)
- [ ] States the prime harvesting questions, and every finding answers one of them (Book p. 34; Brief p. 21)

## Outcome statements

- [ ] Each outcome describes a change in what a social actor does, not an activity or output of the intervention (Brief pp. 2, 16)
- [ ] Each says who changed, what changed, when and where (Brief p. 7)
- [ ] Each gives the contribution: what the change agent did that plausibly influenced the change (Brief pp. 7, 11)
- [ ] Changes in awareness or knowledge appear only with evidence of a change in behaviour (Brief p. 16)
- [ ] The report says contribution, not attribution (Brief p. 16)
- [ ] Unintended and negative outcomes were asked for, and the report says what was found (Brief pp. 14, 17)

## Credibility

- [ ] The report says whether outcomes were substantiated, and why that choice suits the uses (Book pp. 89, 101)
- [ ] If they were: which outcomes, how they were chosen, who substantiated them and what they said (Book pp. 92 to 95)
- [ ] Substantiators were knowledgeable about the outcome and independent of the change agent (Brief p. 18)
- [ ] Outcomes a substantiator found did not happen as described are excluded from the evidence (Book p. 95)

## Findings and use

- [ ] The analysis shows how outcomes were classified, and the categories come from the design (Brief p. 8)
- [ ] The interpretive lens is named (Brief p. 23)
- [ ] Findings answer "so what?" for the users' decisions, and recommendations stay within what the harvesters can know (Brief p. 25)
- [ ] Limits are stated: only outcomes the sources knew about were captured (Brief p. 4)
