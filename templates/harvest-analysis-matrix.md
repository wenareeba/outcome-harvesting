# Harvest analysis matrix

Use this in Step 5, once the outcome statements are final and any substantiation is done. Analysis organises the outcomes into clusters. Interpretation uses those clusters to answer the prime harvesting questions (Book p. 104; Brief p. 21).

Page numbers refer to Wilson-Grau and Britt (2013), the **Brief**, and Wilson-Grau (2019), the **Book**.

## 1. Choose the level of analysis

Tick the level, or levels, the prime questions call for (Brief p. 21):

- [ ] **Each outcome on its own.** Suits outcome statements with rich detail on significance, history or context, which can be told as a story (Brief p. 21).
- [ ] **All the outcomes of one change agent.** How do they add up? Do they reveal a process of change (Brief p. 21)?
- [ ] **An overarching programme with several change agents.** Do their outcomes combine to create broader or deeper change (Brief p. 22)?

## 4. Look for patterns

For each cluster, write one or two sentences:

- Which social actors changed, and how?
- Which contributions of the change agent recur?
- Where are there outcomes the theory of change did not expect, including negative ones?
- Where are the gaps: categories with few or no outcomes, and possible reasons?
