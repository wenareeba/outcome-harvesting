Outcome Harvesting, reference card 7 of 8

Critiques card

The essentials from Lesson 7. "Book" is Wilson-Grau (2019). The critical literature in these sources is thin; there is no systematic review among them.

Bias in what is reported

Only documented or known outcomes are found. INTRAC 2017, p. 4 OH has fewer bias controls than QuIP. Copestake 2017

Responses: on record, SMART, substantiation, separate negative rows, open questions. Book pp. 88 to 89, 178; BetterEvaluation 2024

Open: sources' subjectivity; sources name substantiators; counts are not prevalence. Book pp. 93 to 94, 119, 184

Causal claims

Plausible, inductive, "likely, but not guaranteed". Book p. 181

Responses: contribution, not attribution, by design. Copestake 2017; Abboud and Claussen 2016, p. 52

Open: questions of attribution or efficiency need another method. Book p. 35

Cost and skill

Statements are hard to write; 1 of 6 complete when practitioners drafted alone; records may lack outcomes; budgets slip. INTRAC 2017, p. 4; Abboud and Claussen 2016, pp. 51 to 52; Beardmore et al. 2023, p. 3

Responses: strive for less; harvest periodically; small retrospective harvests work. BetterEvaluation 2024; Beardmore et al. 2023, p. 6

Participation and power

Unclear role for social actors; risk of extraction; power issues for participant substantiators. INTRAC 2017, p. 4; Abboud and Claussen 2016, p. 51

Responses: bring in the people who changed; they enrich statements. Abboud and Claussen 2016, p. 52; Book p. 135

Definition: state whether attitudes count. Book p. 175; BetterEvaluation 2024
