What a harvest is

"The identification, formulation, analysis, and interpretation of outcomes to answer useful questions." Brief p. 2

It collects evidence of what changed, then works backwards to whether and how the intervention contributed. It need not measure progress towards set objectives. Book p. 1

What an outcome is

"A change in the behavior, relationships, actions, activities, policies, or practices of an individual, group, community, organization, or institution." Brief p. 2

Observable, and in the actors the intervention influences, not in beneficiaries' wellbeing. Wellbeing changes are impact. Book pp. 1 to 2

Four roles

  • Change agent: influences an outcome
  • Social actor: changes
  • Harvest user: needs the findings to decide or act
  • Harvester: manages the harvest

Brief p. 4

Six steps

  1. Design the harvest p. 6
  2. Review documentation and draft outcome descriptions p. 10
  3. Engage with change agents to formulate them p. 13
  4. Substantiate p. 18
  5. Analyse and interpret p. 21
  6. Support use of findings p. 25

Where it fits

  • Outcomes, not activities, are what matters Brief p. 2
  • Cause and effect are not well understood Brief p. 2
  • Advocacy, campaigning and policy work Brief p. 3
  • Social change in an unpredictable setting, with unintended outcomes Book p. 5

What it will not tell you

  • Impact on people's lives Book p. 38
  • Value for money on its own Book p. 33
  • Outcomes no source knows about Brief pp. 4, 16

Nine principles

Process. I Facilitate usefulness throughout the harvest. II Coach human sources to formulate outcome statements. III Nurture appropriate participation. IV Strive for less because it will be more useful. V Learn Outcome Harvesting experientially.

Content. VI Harvest social change outcomes. VII Formulate an outcome as an observable change. VIII Establish plausible influence of the intervention. IX Ensure credible-enough outcomes.

Book pp. 14 to 15. Chapters 8 and 9 word some differently; see the bibliography errata.
