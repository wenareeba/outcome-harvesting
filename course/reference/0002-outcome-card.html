Five kinds of change that count

  • Action: something different, once
  • Activity: something different, over time
  • Relationship: actors deal with each other differently
  • Policy: a collective actor changes its rules, norms or laws
  • Practice: a collective actor acts on a new policy

Book p. 170

A qualitative change, something new. Doing more or better usually does not count, unless another actor influenced it. Book p. 171

What does not count, yet

  • Outputs: what the intervention did
  • Knowledge, attitudes, skills: until behaviour changes Book p. 175; Brief p. 16
  • Impact: changes in people's lives or the environment Book p. 171

Context can move the line: attending a workshop can be an outcome if people first had to be persuaded to come. Book p. 176

What does count

  • Not acting, if influenced Book p. 176
  • Negative outcomes that undermine the aim Book p. 177
  • Unintended outcomes Book p. 2

Plausible contribution: two tests

  1. Plausible enough for the uses, in the eyes of user, harvester and substantiator
  2. Specific and measurable enough to verify

Book p. 178

Direct or indirect, intended or not, usually partial. Book p. 179 Contribution, not attribution. Brief p. 16

The outcome statement

  • Outcome: when, who, what they did differently, where
  • Significance: why it matters for the aim
  • Contribution: who did what with whom

Book pp. 38 to 39

Keep different behaviours apart; group dates, actors and places by significance. Book p. 173
