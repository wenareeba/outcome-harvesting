Outcome Harvesting, reference card 6 of 8

Analysis and use card

The essentials from Lesson 6, Steps 5 and 6. "Book" is Wilson-Grau (2019); "Brief" is Wilson-Grau and Britt (2013).

Classify

  • By hand for a small set; a database beyond a few dozen outcomes Book pp. 104 to 106
  • Categories from the questions, tested on the data Book p. 106
  • Four tests: distinct, defined, meaningful, useful Book p. 107
  • Fewer is better; one main category per outcome Book pp. 108, 112

Interpret

  • Answer every prime question, even implicit ones Book p. 114
  • First person; cite outcome numbers and counts in the text Book pp. 118 to 119
  • Outcome and contribution are facts as sources know them; significance is opinion Book pp. 118 to 119
  • Compare only what can be compared Book p. 119
  • Lens: the users' own, or the harvester's expertise Brief pp. 23 to 24

Report

  • About three quarters of the report answers the questions Book p. 123
  • External evaluators correct facts but may keep their interpretation; users can respond Book p. 122
  • Points for discussion, not recommendations Book pp. 124 to 125; Brief p. 25

Support use

  • Usable is not enough; the harvest must be used Book p. 131
  • Findings, process and method Book p. 132
  • Offer: new formats, further analysis, new lenses, others' views. Decline: recommending action Book pp. 134 to 136
  • Watch for symbolic purpose, misuse, abuse, non-use and overuse Book pp. 140 to 142
  • Champion the findings, not the intervention Book p. 145
Sources Wilson-Grau, R. (2019). Outcome harvesting: Principles, steps, and evaluation applications. Information Age Publishing. doi:10.1108/978-1-64113-394-4
Wilson-Grau, R., and Britt, H. (2013). Outcome harvesting. Ford Foundation, Middle East and North Africa Office. May 2012, revised November 2013.
