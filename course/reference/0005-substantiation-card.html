Substantiation card

The essentials from Lesson 5, Step 4. "Book" is Wilson-Grau (2019); "Brief" is Wilson-Grau and Britt (2013).

Whether

The test: is the whole set credible enough for the intended uses? Book p. 88 Not always needed; the harvester helps users decide, and not substantiating must be a conscious choice. Book pp. 88 to 89, p. 101

Bigger claims of change call for more evidence. Brief p. 18

Why

  • Check accuracy of outcome and contribution
  • Deepen and broaden understanding, especially of significance

Book pp. 89 to 90, p. 100

Which and when

  • None, some or all: the uses decide Book pp. 90 to 92
  • Purposive, expert selection by importance and doubt; not a random sample Book p. 93
  • Evaluations: after all outcomes are in. Periodic harvests: once there are enough to judge importance Book p. 89

Who

  • Independent of the outcome and contribution, knowledgeable, authoritative, on record Book p. 94
  • Names come from the original sources Book pp. 93 to 94
  • At least two approached per outcome; one outcome each Book pp. 94 to 95
  • Hard-to-reach leaders: ask someone close to them Book p. 94

Judging replies

  • Ask about outcome and contribution: fully agree, partially agree, disagree, with comments Brief p. 19
  • Not validated: one independent, knowledgeable person finds the outcome or contribution inaccurate Book p. 99
  • Partial agreement with added detail can enrich; when unclear, ask Book p. 98
  • Rule of thumb: at least 90% of those checked validated; failures excluded Book p. 95

Budget by email: 10 to 15 minutes per substantiator, about three weeks overall. Book p. 101

Sources Wilson-Grau, R. (2019). Outcome harvesting: Principles, steps, and evaluation applications. Information Age Publishing. doi:10.1108/978-1-64113-394-4
Wilson-Grau, R., and Britt, H. (2013). Outcome harvesting. Ford Foundation, Middle East and North Africa Office. May 2012, revised November 2013.
