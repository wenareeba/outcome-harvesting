Design card

The essentials from Lesson 3. Page numbers refer to Wilson-Grau (2019).

Six agreements

  1. Primary intended users
  2. Principal uses, of findings and process
  3. Prime harvesting questions
  4. Outcome information needed
  5. Sources: documents, then people
  6. Money, people and time

p. 30. Agreed in principle; revisited as results emerge (pp. 31, 150).

Primary users

Need the findings to decide or act, and take part throughout. Secondary users need the findings but are not involved throughout; the audience is interested. pp. 31 to 32

Three yes answers: worth doing? ready to invest the time? committed to use? p. 31

Uses

Findings: accountability, changing course, planning, engaging allies. Process: staff skills, shared understanding, learning culture. pp. 33 to 34

Best for effectiveness, relevance, sustainability; less for impact; least for efficiency. p. 35

Prime questions

  • Two to five; more means reprioritise p. 35
  • Each asks what, and so what pp. 35 to 36
  • Users want it answered and know how they will use it p. 36
  • Answerable well enough to support action p. 36
  • Needs outcome data p. 38
  • Answerable in time, at reasonable cost p. 38

Rules of thumb for time

  • Design: about 3 days with one user, plus 2 to 3 for instruments p. 44
  • Documents: 5 pages an hour in the text, 7 in the worked example p. 45
  • Sources: about 1 hour per outcome each; about 5 harvester hours per source p. 46
  • Substantiation: half an hour per outcome p. 47
  • Classify 140 outcomes a day; enter 20 an hour p. 47

Estimates only. The commonest mistake is doing too much. pp. 44, 160

Source Wilson-Grau, R. (2019). Outcome harvesting: Principles, steps, and evaluation applications. Information Age Publishing. doi:10.1108/978-1-64113-394-4
