## Where to start

1. Wilson-Grau and Britt (2013), *Outcome harvesting*. The 27-page brief. Free.
2. Wilson-Grau, Britt, Dewata, Rogers and Stevens (2024), BetterEvaluation's *Outcome harvesting* page. A free overview of the steps and principles.
3. Wilson-Grau (2019), *Outcome harvesting: Principles, steps, and evaluation applications*. The full method.

## 1. Foundations: Wilson-Grau's own work

The brief, the book and one applied chapter. Read the brief first, then the book.

- Wilson-Grau, R., and Britt, H. (2013). *Outcome harvesting*. Ford Foundation, Middle East and North Africa Office, Cairo. May 2012, revised November 2013. https://www.betterevaluation.org/tools-resources/outcome-harvesting-0  
  Start here. The 27-page brief that set out the method: the definition of an outcome (p. 2), the four roles in a harvest (p. 4) and the six steps, one section each from p. 6. This repo cites the November 2013 revision; see errata.
- Wilson-Grau, R. (2019). *Outcome harvesting: Principles, steps, and evaluation applications*. Information Age Publishing, Charlotte, NC. Evaluation and Society series. https://doi.org/10.1108/978-1-64113-394-4  
  The full statement of the method. Defines an outcome as an observable change in behaviour (p. 1), gives a chapter to each of the six steps (pp. 29 to 147) and sets out five process principles (pp. 149 to 167) and four content principles (pp. 169 to 187). Copyright 2019; released November 2018. Not open access.

## 2. Guides and adaptations by others

Free, shorter and practical. Useful when you are running a harvest next month.

- Wilson-Grau, R., Britt, H., Dewata, Y., Rogers, P., and Stevens, K. (2024). *Outcome harvesting*. BetterEvaluation, approach page. Original author R. Wilson-Grau; revised by H. Britt, Y. Dewata, P. Rogers and K. Stevens. Last updated 27 June 2024. https://www.betterevaluation.org/methods-approaches/approaches/outcome-harvesting  
  Free overview of the six steps and the nine principles, with links to guides and examples. Its principle names follow Chapter 1 of the book, except one; see errata.
- World Bank (2014a). *Outcome-based learning field guide: Tools to harvest and monitor outcomes and systematically learn from complex projects*. World Bank, Washington, DC. Working Paper 90176. https://doi.org/10.1596/20006  
  Practical tools built from pilots with World Bank teams. Its outcome definition is narrower than the brief: a change in the people, groups, communities or organisations a programme works with directly (p. 9). Open access.
- INTRAC (2017). *Outcome harvesting*. M&E Universe, INTRAC. https://www.intrac.org/wpcms/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/Outcome-harvesting.pdf  
  Five-page summary of the steps with a plain list of strengths and weaknesses (p. 3 onwards). Free.

## 3. Method development, comparison and critique

Where others adapt OH, test it, or compare it with other approaches. A small literature so far.

- Abboud, R., and Claussen, C. (2016). The use of Outcome Harvesting in learning-oriented and collaborative inquiry approaches to evaluation: An example from Calgary, Alberta. *Evaluation and Program Planning*, 59, 47 to 54. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.evalprogplan.2016.08.005  
  OH used as a shared learning process across neighbourhood community development projects in Calgary, from 2013, with lessons for others adopting it (p. 47).
- Beardmore, A., Jones, M., and Seal, J. (2023). Outcome harvesting as a methodology for the retrospective evaluation of small-scale community development interventions. *Evaluation and Program Planning*, 97, 102235. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.evalprogplan.2023.102235  
  Tests OH on a place-based community wellbeing project. Finds it promising, with two limits: reaching a broad range of participants, and finding enough time and resources (p. 1). Open access, CC BY 4.0.
- Copestake, J. (2017). *A family resemblance: Outcome Harvesting and QuIP compared*. Bath Social and Development Research, blog post, 4 July 2017. https://bathsdr.org/782-2/  
  Short comparison of OH with the Qualitative Impact Protocol, from the QuIP side.

## 4. Applications

Published harvests, with Africa, Asia and Latin America sought out.

- World Bank (2014b). *Cases in outcome harvesting: Ten pilot experiences identify new learning from multi-stakeholder projects to improve results*. World Bank, Washington, DC. Working Paper 90172. https://doi.org/10.1596/20015  
  Ten pilot harvests in World Bank projects, among them pharmaceutical procurement in Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda (p. 6). Open access.
- Smith, R., Scheers, G., and Robinson, G. (2020). *Outcome harvesting evaluation of 20 years of cooperation between the Danish and Ugandan Disability Movements, 1996-2018*. Disabled Peoples Organisations Denmark (DPOD). Final report, November 2020. https://www.outcomeharvesting.net/oh-evaluation-reports  
  A full harvest from Uganda, from design to outcome database, with its adaptation of the six steps and its substantiation responses in annexes (p. 3).
- Tomei, G., Terenzi, L., and Testi, E. (2024). Using Outcome Harvesting to evaluate socio-economic development and social innovation generated by Social Enterprises in complex areas. The case of BADAEL project in Lebanon. *Evaluation and Program Planning*, 106, 102475. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.evalprogplan.2024.102475  
  OH used to evaluate social enterprise development in Lebanon. Details confirmed on the journal and RePEc records; not yet read for this course.
- Rassmann, K. (2013). *52 weeks of BetterEvaluation: Week 16: Identifying and documenting emergent outcomes of a global network*. BetterEvaluation, blog post, 12 April 2013. https://betterevaluation.org/node/2414  
  The blog that introduces the BioNET harvest paper: why a logframe did not fit a global network, and what the harvest found.

## 5. Related approaches

Outcome Mapping, Most Significant Change, QuIP and contribution analysis.

- Davies, R., and Dart, J. (2005). *The 'Most Significant Change' (MSC) technique: A guide to its use*. Version 1.00, April 2005. https://susana.org/knowledge-hub/resources?id=4568  
  The standard guide to MSC, the other approach that collects stories of change without fixing indicators in advance. Free.
- Copestake, J., Morsink, M., and Remnant, F. (2019). *Attributing development impact: The Qualitative Impact Protocol case book*. Practical Action Publishing. https://doi.org/10.3362/9781780447469  
  Cases using QuIP, which asks intended beneficiaries about change without naming the intervention. Read beside Copestake (2017). Open access.
- Mayne, J. (2019). Revisiting contribution analysis. *Canadian Journal of Program Evaluation*, 34(2), 171 to 191. https://doi.org/10.3138/cjpe.68004  
  The current statement of contribution analysis, the method this course pairs with OH: harvest the outcomes, then test the important ones.

## 6. Complexity-aware monitoring

Why methods like OH exist.

- Britt, H. (2013). *Discussion note: Complexity-aware monitoring*. USAID, Monitoring and Evaluation Series, Version 2.0, December 2013. https://www.betterevaluation.org/sites/default/files/Complexity_Aware_Monitoring_2013-12-11_FINAL.pdf  
  Places OH among five complexity-aware monitoring approaches (p. 7) and explains why such methods look for unintended as well as intended results (p. 11).
